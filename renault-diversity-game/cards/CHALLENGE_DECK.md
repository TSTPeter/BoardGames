# Challenge Deck - Renault D&I Game

Each Challenge card represents a real workplace scenario related to diversity and inclusion at Renault. Challenges are ordered by difficulty and introduce different game mechanics and constraints.

## How to Read Challenge Cards

**Challenge Title:** Descriptive name
**Scenario:** Real D&I situation this represents
**Win Condition:** What the team must accomplish
**Special Rules:** Any modifications to standard gameplay
**Difficulty:** ★ (Beginner) to ★★★★★ (Expert)

---

## BEGINNER CHALLENGES (1-10)

### Challenge 1: Building the Foundation
**Difficulty:** ★
**Scenario:** Starting your D&I journey - everyone contributes

**Win Condition:**
- Each player must win at least one trick
- Complete all cards in hand

**Special Rules:** None (standard communication and trick-taking)

**Facilitator Note:** This introduces basic cooperation and ensures everyone participates. Relates to the principle that D&I requires everyone's contribution, not just designated champions.

---

### Challenge 2: The Quiet Voices
**Difficulty:** ★
**Scenario:** Ensuring everyone has a voice in meetings, especially those who typically speak less

**Win Condition:**
- The player who speaks first in real life today must win the fewest tricks
- The player who typically speaks least must win the most tricks

**Special Rules:**
- Before starting, team identifies who spoke first today and who typically speaks least
- Standard communication allowed

**Facilitator Note:** Highlights how we can intentionally create space for quieter voices and the value they bring.

---

### Challenge 3: Cross-Cultural Collaboration
**Difficulty:** ★★
**Scenario:** Working across cultural communication styles

**Win Condition:**
- Players must alternate winning tricks by suit color (Red, Black, Red, Black...)
- Complete at least 8 tricks in this pattern

**Special Rules:**
- First trick winner determines starting color
- Pattern must be maintained

**Facilitator Note:** Represents the rhythm and balance needed when bridging different cultural approaches.

---

### Challenge 4: Limited Resources
**Difficulty:** ★★
**Scenario:** When D&I initiatives have constrained budgets and headcount

**Win Condition:**
- Remove the player with the most cards from the game after dealing
- Remaining players must win exactly 5 tricks total
- Each remaining player must win at least 1 trick

**Special Rules:**
- Removed player becomes advisor (can observe but not play)
- Advisor may answer yes/no questions only

**Facilitator Note:** Demonstrates how losing diversity makes tasks harder. The "advisor" role shows how excluded voices can still contribute if asked.

---

### Challenge 5: The Hiring Panel
**Difficulty:** ★★
**Scenario:** Ensuring balanced representation in hiring decisions

**Win Condition:**
- Tricks must be won by different players in this exact sequence:
  - Player A wins trick 1
  - Player B wins trick 2
  - Player C wins trick 3
  - (Pattern repeats)
- Complete at least 6 tricks

**Special Rules:**
- Team chooses the player order (A, B, C...) before starting
- Standard communication allowed

**Facilitator Note:** Represents intentional sequencing to ensure all voices are heard in decisions. Like a hiring panel, success requires planning who contributes when.

---

### Challenge 6: Communication Breakdown
**Difficulty:** ★★
**Scenario:** When language barriers or communication styles create misunderstandings

**Win Condition:**
- Win exactly 7 tricks as a team
- At least 3 different players must win tricks

**Special Rules:**
- **NO verbal communication allowed during play**
- Players may use only gestures, facial expressions, or eye contact
- Standard trick-taking rules apply

**Facilitator Note:** Experience the frustration of limited communication and the creativity needed to overcome it. Relates to working with colleagues who communicate differently.

---

### Challenge 7: The Generation Gap
**Difficulty:** ★★★
**Scenario:** Bridging different generational perspectives and work styles

**Win Condition:**
- Players must win tricks in age order (youngest to oldest) for first 3 tricks
- Then reverse order (oldest to youngest) for next 3 tricks
- Complete the pattern successfully

**Special Rules:**
- Before starting, players arrange themselves by age
- May discuss strategy for 1 minute before play
- Limited communication during play (gestures only)

**Facilitator Note:** Represents how different generations bring different strengths. Success requires intentionally leveraging both emerging and experienced perspectives.

---

### Challenge 8: Unconscious Bias
**Difficulty:** ★★★
**Scenario:** When assumptions and biases affect decision-making

**Win Condition:**
- Before dealing, each player predicts which suit they'll be "strongest" in
- During play, players must win at least one trick with their "weakest" suit (the suit they have fewest cards in)
- Team must win exactly 8 tricks total

**Special Rules:**
- After seeing their cards, players write down their weakest suit
- Standard communication about strategy allowed
- Players must consciously use cards they assumed would be weak

**Facilitator Note:** Challenges assumptions about strength and weakness. Like unconscious bias, our first impressions may not reflect actual capability.

---

### Challenge 9: The Promotion Cycle
**Difficulty:** ★★★
**Scenario:** Ensuring fair access to advancement opportunities

**Win Condition:**
- Identify the player with the "weakest" starting hand (fewest high cards)
- That player must win the first trick AND the last trick
- Team must win at least 6 tricks total

**Special Rules:**
- Team has 2 minutes to examine hands and identify "weakest" player
- Players may share general information about their hands
- Player abilities may be used to strengthen the identified player

**Facilitator Note:** Represents affirmative action - deliberately supporting those who start with disadvantages. Team must work together to enable their success.

---

### Challenge 10: Building Belonging
**Difficulty:** ★★★
**Scenario:** Creating a culture where everyone feels they belong

**Win Condition:**
- Every player must win at least 2 tricks
- No player may win more than 4 tricks
- Complete all cards successfully

**Special Rules:**
- Players may communicate freely before each trick
- Must announce "I need support" or "I can help" before playing
- Team must actively balance contributions

**Facilitator Note:** Belonging means everyone contributes meaningfully without anyone dominating. Success requires active monitoring and adjustment.

---

## ADVANCED CHALLENGES (11-20)

### Challenge 11: Remote Work Equity
**Difficulty:** ★★★★
**Scenario:** Ensuring remote workers aren't disadvantaged

**Win Condition:**
- One player is designated "remote" and sits apart from the team
- Remote player must win exactly 3 tricks
- Other players must collectively win exactly 6 tricks
- Remote player can only communicate through written notes

**Special Rules:**
- Remote player cannot speak or hear others
- Team may pass written notes (max 5 words) once per trick
- Standard trick-taking rules

**Facilitator Note:** Highlights the intentional effort needed to include remote team members equitably.

---

### Challenge 12: Intersectionality
**Difficulty:** ★★★★
**Scenario:** Understanding multiple, overlapping identity dimensions

**Win Condition:**
- Each trick must be won with a card that shares NO characteristics with the previous winning card:
  - Different suit AND different number type (odd/even)
- Complete 6 tricks in this pattern

**Special Rules:**
- First winning card is chosen freely
- Subsequent winning cards must differ in both suit and odd/even
- Team must plan carefully

**Facilitator Note:** Represents how people have multiple, intersecting identities. Solutions must consider complexity, not simple categories.

---

### Challenge 13: The Microaggression
**Difficulty:** ★★★★
**Scenario:** When small, cumulative slights impact team members

**Win Condition:**
- Each time a player cannot follow suit, they lose one card permanently (removed from game)
- Team must still win exactly 5 tricks
- All remaining players must win at least one trick

**Special Rules:**
- When you can't follow suit, discard your played card plus one additional card of your choice
- Tracks the cumulative impact of being "left out"
- More challenging as the game progresses

**Facilitator Note:** Demonstrates how small exclusions compound. Each time someone can't participate (follow suit), they lose resources (cards), making future participation harder.

---

### Challenge 14: Parental Leave Transition
**Difficulty:** ★★★★
**Scenario:** Supporting team members returning from parental leave

**Win Condition:**
- After trick 3, one player must sit out for 2 tricks (on "leave")
- When they return, they must win the very next trick
- Team must support their success
- Complete 8 tricks total

**Special Rules:**
- Team chooses who goes "on leave"
- During leave, that player cannot communicate
- Team may share cards with returning player (using abilities)

**Facilitator Note:** Represents the challenge of re-entry after leave and the team support needed for successful transitions.

---

### Challenge 15: Pay Equity Analysis
**Difficulty:** ★★★★
**Scenario:** Identifying and addressing compensation disparities

**Win Condition:**
- After dealing, identify the player with the lowest total card value
- That player must end the round with the HIGHEST score (most tricks won)
- All other players must win exactly the same number of tricks as each other

**Special Rules:**
- Team may examine all hands openly
- Players may trade cards before play begins (using abilities)
- Requires careful calculation and redistribution

**Facilitator Note:** Represents corrective action for inequity. Team must deliberately redistribute resources to address imbalances.

---

### Challenge 16: The Dominant Voice
**Difficulty:** ★★★★★
**Scenario:** When one perspective or group dominates discussions

**Win Condition:**
- Identify the player with the most high cards (10-13)
- That player may NOT win any tricks
- All other players must each win at least 2 tricks
- Complete 8 tricks successfully

**Special Rules:**
- Dominant player may communicate and play strategically
- They must intentionally lose while helping others win
- Tests restraint and supportive behavior

**Facilitator Note:** Sometimes inclusion means those with privilege must actively step back. The "dominant" player must use their strength to enable others.

---

### Challenge 17: Disability Accommodation
**Difficulty:** ★★★★★
**Scenario:** Adapting processes to be genuinely accessible

**Win Condition:**
- One player must play their cards face-up (visible to all)
- Another player must play without looking at their own cards (face-down)
- Team must win exactly 6 tricks
- Both constrained players must win at least 1 trick each

**Special Rules:**
- Face-up player: No privacy but can plan openly
- Face-down player: Selects cards at random, team must guide them
- Team must adapt strategy to both constraints

**Facilitator Note:** Different people need different accommodations. True accessibility means adapting your approach, not just "treating everyone the same."

---

### Challenge 18: Recruitment Pipeline
**Difficulty:** ★★★★★
**Scenario:** Building diverse talent pipelines over time

**Win Condition:**
- Play in 3 rounds with the same challenge
- Round 1: Play with full team
- Round 2: Add one previously excluded player (if available) or give each player +2 cards
- Round 3: Team must win more tricks than Round 1
- Demonstrates improvement through diversity

**Special Rules:**
- Track tricks won in each round
- Between rounds, team may discuss strategy for 1 minute
- Communication limited during play (gestures only)

**Facilitator Note:** Diversity benefits compound over time. Initial investment creates stronger future performance.

---

### Challenge 19: Psychological Safety
**Difficulty:** ★★★★★
**Scenario:** Creating an environment where people can take risks

**Win Condition:**
- Each player must intentionally lose at least one trick they COULD have won
- Team must collectively win exactly 7 tricks
- Players must announce "I'm taking a risk" when deliberately losing

**Special Rules:**
- Requires vulnerability and trust
- Team must support risk-taking without punishment
- Careful balance between risk and success

**Facilitator Note:** Psychological safety means you can afford to fail. Team must create conditions where strategic "failure" enables overall success.

---

### Challenge 20: Systemic Change
**Difficulty:** ★★★★★
**Scenario:** Transforming organizational systems and culture

**Win Condition:**
- Combine elements from 3 previous challenges (facilitator chooses)
- Team must complete all three win conditions simultaneously
- Represents addressing multiple systemic issues at once

**Special Rules:**
- Facilitator selects 3 previous challenges
- All win conditions and special rules apply concurrently
- Most difficult challenge - requires mastery of all concepts

**Facilitator Note:** Real systemic change requires addressing multiple dimensions simultaneously. Success requires everything learned in the game so far.

---

## Challenge Deck Design Notes

### Progressive Difficulty
- Challenges 1-5: Learn basic cooperation
- Challenges 6-10: Introduce constraints and special conditions
- Challenges 11-15: Complex scenarios requiring advanced planning
- Challenges 16-20: Expert-level systemic challenges

### D&I Themes Covered
- Voice and participation (Challenges 2, 7, 16)
- Communication barriers (Challenges 6, 11)
- Resource constraints (Challenges 4, 13)
- Equity and fairness (Challenges 9, 15)
- Belonging and inclusion (Challenges 10, 19)
- Systemic change (Challenges 12, 18, 20)
- Accommodation (Challenge 17)
- Intersectionality (Challenge 12)

### Customization for Renault
Facilitators should adapt challenge scenarios with specific Renault examples during discussion.

### Replayability
- Challenges can be played in different orders
- Same challenge becomes different with different player abilities
- Teams can compete to see who progresses furthest
