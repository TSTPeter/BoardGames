# Facilitator Guide - Renault D&I Cooperative Card Game

## Welcome, Facilitator!

This guide will help you successfully run the Renault Diversity & Inclusion game. This is an experiential learning tool designed to build D&I awareness, identify inclusive behaviors, and enable personal commitments to action.

**Your Role:** Create a safe, engaging learning environment where participants connect game experiences to real workplace dynamics.

---

## Quick Reference

**Total Session Time:** 90-120 minutes
**Players:** 3-6 per table (can run multiple tables in parallel)
**Components Needed:** Playing cards, Challenge deck, Gold deck, Player Ability cards, score tracker
**Learning Outcomes:** Awareness, identification, application, commitment

---

## Pre-Session Preparation

### 1. Materials Checklist (Per Table)

**Required:**
- [ ] 1 standard 52-card playing deck (or custom Renault deck)
- [ ] Challenge Deck cards (printed and ordered/shuffled based on difficulty chosen)
- [ ] Gold Deck cards (printed and ordered)
- [ ] Player Ability cards (6 cards, shuffled)
- [ ] Score tracking sheet or tokens
- [ ] Playing surface (table with space for all players)

**Optional:**
- [ ] Suit reference cards (explaining Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades themes)
- [ ] Individual reflection worksheets
- [ ] Timer (for time-limited discussions)
- [ ] Flip chart or whiteboard for capturing insights

### 2. Room Setup

**Physical Arrangement:**
- Round tables for 4-6 players (promotes equal participation)
- Facilitator able to observe all tables
- Adequate lighting
- Minimal distractions

**For Multiple Tables:**
- Space tables apart enough for separate conversations
- Central area for facilitator instructions
- Way to get all tables' attention (bell, raised hand signal)

### 3. Facilitator Preparation

**Know Your Content:**
- Read all game rules thoroughly
- Understand each Challenge card
- Review Gold Card discussion questions
- Familiarize yourself with Player Abilities

**Know Your Audience:**
- What's the participants' current D&I awareness level?
- What's happening at Renault that makes D&I relevant right now?
- Any recent initiatives or challenges to reference?
- Cultural sensitivities to be aware of?

**Set Your Intentions:**
- What are the top 3 learning outcomes you want?
- What behavior changes would you like to see?
- How will you handle resistance or defensiveness?

## Detailed Session Plan

### PART 1: Welcome & Setup (10-15 minutes)

#### Opening (3 minutes)

**Welcome Script (Customize for your context):**

"Welcome to the Renault Diversity & Inclusion game experience. Over the next [X] minutes, we're going to play a cooperative card game that will help us explore what it takes to build a truly inclusive workplace.

This isn't a traditional training session. You'll learn by experiencing challenges that mirror real D&I dynamics. The game is designed to be both engaging and thought-provoking.

Our objectives today are to:
1. Build awareness of workplace diversity and inclusion
2. Identify inclusive vs. non-inclusive behaviors
3. Connect D&I to your specific work context
4. Enable each of you to commit to at least one personal behavior change

There are no wrong moves in this game - only learning opportunities."

#### Set the Container (2 minutes)

**Creating Safety:**

"A few guidelines for our time together:
- **Respect:** All perspectives and experiences are valid
- **Curiosity:** Approach with openness, not judgment
- **Confidentiality:** What's shared here stays here
- **Participation:** Everyone's voice matters
- **Learning Stance:** We're all here to learn, including me

Questions or concerns before we begin?"

#### Game Overview (5 minutes)

**Explain the Core Concept:**

"This is a cooperative trick-taking card game. You'll work as a team to complete challenges.

**The Basic Mechanics:**
- Each round, you'll attempt to complete a Challenge based on a real Renault D&I scenario
- You'll play cards to the center in 'tricks'
- The team wins or loses together
- As you progress, you'll discuss D&I concepts through 'Gold Cards'

**Key Point:** The game is asymmetrical - each of you will have a different player ability, meaning you'll all approach the game differently. This mirrors how diverse teams work.

I'll teach you the specific rules as we set up. It's easier to learn by playing than by listening to all rules upfront."

#### Optional: Dispersed Rule Learning (8 minutes)

**If using this variant:**

"We're going to learn the rules together. I'm going to give each of you one aspect of the game to explain to the others. This is intentional - just like in diverse teams, we'll each bring different knowledge and teach each other."

1. Assign rule sections to players:
   - Player 1: Basic trick-taking rules
   - Player 2: Following suit and winning tricks
   - Player 3: Communication rules
   - Player 4: Player abilities
   - (etc.)

2. Give players 2 minutes to read their section
3. Each player teaches their part (1 minute each)
4. Facilitator clarifies and corrects as needed

**Debrief the experience:** "How did it feel to each be responsible for different knowledge? This is how diverse expertise works."

---

### PART 2: Gameplay (40-60 minutes)

#### Setup (5 minutes)

**Distribute Components:**
1. Give each table their materials
2. Shuffle and deal Player Ability cards (one per player, face up)
3. Each player reads and understands their ability
4. Place Challenge Deck and Gold Deck where all can see

**First Challenge:**
1. Reveal Challenge 1
2. Read it aloud
3. Ensure everyone understands the win condition
4. Deal playing cards
5. Begin!

#### Facilitator Role During Play

**DO:**
- Circulate between tables (if multiple)
- Observe dynamics and interactions
- Mental note of learning moments to reference later
- Answer rules questions
- Keep energy positive
- Encourage teams that are struggling ("This challenge is tough - what could you try differently?")
- Celebrate successes
- Ensure Challenge and Gold Card discussions happen

**DON'T:**
- Tell teams what to do strategically
- Over-facilitate - let them struggle productively
- Interrupt gameplay frequently
- Make it competitive between tables (it's cooperative)
- Let one table fall too far behind (adjust difficulty if needed)

#### Managing Gold Card Discussions (5-8 minutes per card)

**When a team completes a milestone challenge (2, 4, 6, 8, 10...):**

1. **Pause Gameplay:** "Great job! You've reached a milestone. Time for a Gold Card."

2. **Read the Card:** Read the entire Gold Card aloud, slowly

3. **Give Reflection Time:** "Take 30 seconds to think about this concept and how it showed up in the game just now."

4. **Facilitate Discussion:**
   - Use the questions on the Gold Card
   - Ensure everyone speaks (go around the table)
   - Connect to specific game moments: "Remember when..."
   - Start to bridge to real work: "How might this relate to your actual team?"

5. **Capture Insights:** Note key points on flip chart (optional but powerful)

6. **Resume Play:** "Let's take that learning into the next challenge."

**Pacing:**
- Keep Gold Card discussions to 5-8 minutes
- Balance depth with maintaining gameplay momentum
- Save deepest discussion for final debrief

#### Managing Challenge Failures

**When a team fails a challenge:**

"You didn't complete the challenge - and that's okay. This is a learning moment.

- What made that challenge difficult?
- What would you do differently?
- What does this teach you about [the D&I concept in that challenge]?

You can either:
- Try this challenge again with your learning
- Move forward and count it as your final score

What would you like to do?"

**Most teams benefit from trying once more, then moving on.**

#### Adjusting Difficulty On-the-Fly

**If challenges are too easy:**
- Skip to higher-numbered challenges
- Add additional constraints
- Reduce communication further

**If challenges are too hard:**
- Allow more communication than challenge specifies
- Let team retry with more planning time
- Use easier challenges

**Remember:** The goal is learning, not perfect gameplay. Adjust to keep it engaging.

---

### PART 3: Debrief & Application (30-40 minutes)

This is where the real learning happens. The game creates experiences; the debrief creates meaning.

#### Immediate Reactions (5 minutes)

**Open with:**
"Let's pause and reflect. First, just reactions - how are you feeling? What's standing out?"

**Go around the table/room:**
- Let everyone share briefly
- Don't analyze yet, just air reactions
- Acknowledge emotions (frustration, enjoyment, surprise)

#### Structured Reflection (15 minutes)

**Use a progression of questions that move from game → workplace → action:**

**Level 1: Game Experience**
1. "What moments in the game were most challenging? Why?"
2. "When did your team work together most effectively?"
3. "How did having different abilities affect your team's success?"
4. "What did you learn about cooperation under constraints?"

**Level 2: D&I Connections**
5. "Which challenge felt most like a real workplace situation you've experienced?"
6. "What did the game help you understand about inclusion that you didn't before?"
7. "Which Gold Card concept resonated most with you? Why?"
8. "How did the communication restrictions mirror real workplace barriers?"

**Level 3: Personal Application**
9. "Think about the 'suit' you had least of (Hearts/Diamonds/Clubs/Spades). What does that tell you about your experience at Renault?"
10. "Where do you see yourself in the game - which player ability reflects your real strength?"
11. "What inclusive behavior could you do more of?"
12. "What non-inclusive behavior might you unintentionally engage in?"

**Facilitation Tips:**
- Give thinking time before people answer
- Encourage specificity: "Can you give an example?"
- Make it safe to be vulnerable: "That takes courage to share"
- Connect people's contributions: "That relates to what [name] said about..."
- Don't let the conversation be dominated by a few voices

#### Activity: Personal Reflection (5 minutes)

**Individual Writing:**

"I'm going to give you 5 minutes of quiet reflection time. Use the worksheet [or blank paper] to answer:

1. **One D&I insight I'm taking away:**
2. **One behavior I will START doing:**
3. **One behavior I will STOP doing:**
4. **One conversation I need to have:**

Be specific. Instead of 'I'll be more inclusive,' try 'I'll ensure the two quiet team members speak before I share my opinion in our Monday meetings.'"

**Why This Works:**
- Writing commits them to the learning
- Specificity enables action
- Personal ownership vs. abstract agreement

#### Sharing Commitments (10 minutes)

**Go around the room:**

"Who's willing to share one commitment they're making?"

**As facilitator:**
- Thank each person for sharing
- Ask clarifying questions: "How will you remember to do that?" "What might get in the way?"
- Invite others to support: "How could the team help [name] with that commitment?"
- Capture commitments on flip chart (creates social accountability)

**Optional - Accountability Partners:**
"Find one other person in this room. Exchange one commitment each. You're now accountability partners. Check in with each other in two weeks."

---

### PART 4: Closing (5-10 minutes)

#### Synthesize Key Learnings (3 minutes)

**Facilitator Summary:**

"Let me share what I heard today..." [Summarize 3-5 key themes that emerged]

"The game helped us experience:
- [Key insight 1]
- [Key insight 2]
- [Key insight 3]

More importantly, you've each committed to specific actions. Those commitments - taken together - can shift our culture."

#### Next Steps (2 minutes)

**Provide Resources:**
- "Here are resources on D&I at Renault: [list]"
- "If you want to explore today's concepts more: [readings, trainings, contacts]"
- "Our next D&I initiative is: [upcoming opportunity]"

**Action Items:**
- "Share your commitments with your manager in your next 1-on-1"
- "Bring one insight to your team meeting"
- "Schedule a team discussion about 'the four suits' in your context"

#### Gratitude and Closing (2 minutes)

"Thank you for engaging authentically today. D&I work requires vulnerability and courage, and you brought both.

Remember: Inclusion isn't something the company does TO you - it's something we all create TOGETHER through our daily choices.

I'm excited to see the impact of your commitments.

Thank you."

**Optional:** Invite final words or questions.

## Facilitation Skills Guide

### Creating Psychological Safety

**Why It Matters:**
D&I discussions can trigger defensiveness, vulnerability, or discomfort. People won't learn if they don't feel safe.

**How to Create Safety:**

1. **Model Vulnerability:**
   - Share your own D&I learning journey
   - Admit when you don't know something
   - Acknowledge complexity and discomfort

2. **Normalize Struggle:**
   - "This is hard stuff - it's supposed to be challenging"
   - "If you're feeling uncomfortable, that's actually where learning happens"
   - "There's no test at the end - we're all learning"

3. **Respond to Mistakes with Curiosity:**
   - If someone says something problematic: "Help me understand what you mean by that"
   - Not: "That's offensive"
   - But: "I wonder if that assumption might not hold for everyone's experience"

4. **Protect Vulnerable Voices:**
   - If someone shares something personal and another person dismisses it: "Hold on - let's really hear what [name] is saying"
   - "Different people have very different experiences - all are valid"

5. **Manage Dominant Voices:**
   - "Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet"
   - "I appreciate your perspective, [name]. I also want to make sure we hear from others"

### Handling Resistance or Defensiveness

**You May Encounter:**
- "This is just political correctness gone mad"
- "We're hiring the best person for the job - diversity would mean lowering standards"
- "I don't see color/gender/etc. - I treat everyone the same"
- "Reverse discrimination"
- Silence/disengagement

**How to Respond:**

1. **Acknowledge the Feeling:**
   - "I hear that you're frustrated"
   - "This topic brings up strong reactions - that's normal"

2. **Seek to Understand:**
   - "Say more about that concern"
   - "What experience led you to that conclusion?"

3. **Reframe:**
   - "What if we thought about it this way..."
   - "The research actually shows..."
   - "Let me offer a different perspective..."

4. **Return to Shared Goal:**
   - "We all want [better performance / stronger teams / fair workplace]. We might disagree on how to get there, but can we agree on that goal?"

5. **Invoke the Game Experience:**
   - "Remember when the player with fewer cards struggled to participate? That's what systemic barriers feel like"
   - "You experienced in the game how different abilities were all valuable - same is true with diversity"

6. **Know Your Limits:**
   - You don't have to convince everyone in the moment
   - Plant seeds; change happens over time
   - If someone is being disruptive: "Let's take this offline" or "I appreciate your engagement and also need to make sure we hear other perspectives"

### Encouraging Participation

**For Quiet Participants:**
- Direct invitations: "[Name], I'd love to hear your thoughts on this"
- Small group share first, then whole group
- Written reflection before verbal sharing
- Go around the circle so everyone speaks

**For Dominant Participants:**
- "Thank you, [name]. Let's hear from someone else now"
- Establish norms: "Everyone shares once before anyone shares twice"
- Private conversation during break: "I appreciate your engagement. I also want to make sure others have space"

**For the Group:**
- "Let's hear from someone who has a different perspective"
- "Who else has experienced something similar?"
- "Before we move on, anyone have a different take?"

### Reading the Room

**Energy Levels:**
- **High energy:** Ride it, but channel it productively
- **Low energy:** Take a movement break, shift activities, check in
- **Tense energy:** Name it ("I'm sensing some tension"), provide space

**Emotional Responses:**
- **Strong emotion (anger, sadness):** "This is bringing up real feelings. That means it matters."
- **Laughter/Deflection:** Sometimes this is comfort; sometimes it's avoidance. Notice which.
- **Shutdown/Disengagement:** Check in individually or adjust approach

**Pacing:**
- If discussion is rich: extend it, shorten something else
- If flat: move on, try different activity
- Trust your instincts

## Adapting for Different Contexts

### For D&I Novices
- Spend more time on setup and context
- Use more Gold Card discussion time
- Provide clear frameworks and vocabulary
- Be patient with basic questions
- Celebrate "aha" moments explicitly

### For D&I Experienced Groups
- Move faster through concepts they know
- Go deeper on application
- Challenge them with harder questions
- Invite them to share their knowledge
- Focus on "what's next" not "what is"

### For Leadership Teams
- Emphasize systemic change and accountability
- Connect to business outcomes
- Focus on "What will you do differently?" not just "What do you think?"
- Discuss modeling and culture-setting
- Plan for cascading learning to their teams

### For Cross-Functional Teams
- Highlight how different functions experience D&I differently
- Discuss silos and information sharing
- Explore how handoffs affect inclusion
- Build cross-functional empathy

### For New Teams
- Use game as team-building, not just D&I learning
- Emphasize the cooperation and communication
- Help them learn each other's strengths
- Create early positive shared experience

### For Experienced Teams
- Reference their shared history
- Connect to past team challenges
- Ask them to hold each other accountable
- Use as a reset or re-commitment moment

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Issue: Rules confusion during play
**Solution:**
- Pause and clarify
- Demonstrate with example
- Let minor errors slide if they don't affect learning
- Remember: learning D&I is the goal, perfect gameplay isn't

### Issue: One team finishing much faster/slower than others
**Solution:**
- Faster team: Give them a harder challenge or ask them to debrief while waiting
- Slower team: Simplify their current challenge or give more communication freedom
- It's okay if teams are at different spots

### Issue: Team failing repeatedly and getting frustrated
**Solution:**
- Reframe failure as learning
- Ask: "What's this teaching you?"
- Allow them to adjust difficulty
- Connect frustration to real inclusion challenges: "How might this frustration feel familiar?"

### Issue: Shallow discussion / people not engaging deeply
**Solution:**
- Ask more probing follow-up questions
- Share a personal example to model vulnerability
- Use smaller breakout groups
- Do written reflection to give thinking time
- Check if psychological safety is the issue

### Issue: Conversation dominated by 1-2 people
**Solution:**
- "Let's hear from [quiet person]"
- "Everyone shares once before anyone shares twice"
- Use round-robin sharing
- Private word with dominant person during break

### Issue: Someone makes an offensive comment
**Solution:**
- Don't ignore it
- Don't attack the person
- Seek understanding: "Help me understand what you mean"
- Offer alternative: "Here's another way to think about that"
- Name impact if needed: "That language can be hurtful because..."
- Move forward while holding the line: "Let's keep our discussion respectful"

### Issue: Running out of time
**Solution:**
- Prioritize debrief over completing all challenges
- Score matters less than learning
- Shorten gameplay, extend discussion
- Send reflection questions for individual completion later

### Issue: Too much time remaining
**Solution:**
- Go deeper on Gold Card discussions
- Add challenge rounds
- Facilitate suit-based reflection activity
- Break into smaller groups for specific discussions
- Action planning

## Final Thoughts for Facilitators

**This game is a tool, not the solution.** It creates experiences that open minds and hearts. The real work happens after the game - in daily choices, conversations, and commitments.

**You don't need to be perfect.** You need to be authentic, curious, and committed to learning alongside participants.

**Trust the process.** The game mechanics naturally surface D&I dynamics. Your job is to help people name and reflect on what they're experiencing.

**Make it your own.** Use this guide as a foundation, but adapt it to your style, your participants, and Renault's specific context.

**The magic is in the discussion, not the gameplay.** Don't get overly focused on rules perfection. Focus on creating meaningful conversations.

**You're planting seeds.** Some will sprout immediately, some will take time, some may never grow. That's okay. Keep planting.

**Thank you for facilitating this work.** D&I facilitation requires skill, courage, and heart. You're making a difference.
